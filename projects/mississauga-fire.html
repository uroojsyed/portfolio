Professional Work / Municipal Digital Experience / 2023–2024

City of Mississauga — Fire Safety Digital Experience

Improving access to municipal fire-safety information through user research, information architecture, content strategy and accessible website design.

Whimsical WordPress User Research Web Analytics Information Architecture Content Audit UX Design AODA Content Strategy CMS

Project overview

As part of the City of Mississauga's annual Fire Safety Campaign, I worked on improving how fire prevention and safety information was organized and presented on the City's website.

The project focused on making public information easier to locate, understand and navigate. This involved reviewing existing website content, identifying usability issues, developing a more intuitive information architecture and supporting the implementation of updated pages and navigation.

My contributions combined web analytics, user feedback, content auditing, user-flow development, cross-functional collaboration and website content management.

The challenge

Fire-safety information needs to be clear, accurate and accessible to residents looking for guidance on prevention and safety. The challenge was to improve how this information was categorized and presented so visitors could navigate to relevant content more intuitively.

Design question: How can we organize fire-safety information so the content is intuitive, accessible and easy to navigate?

Project scope

The work addressed both the organization of existing information and the experience of navigating it across the municipal website.

  1. Evaluate existing content and identify opportunities to improve usability.
  2. Review web analytics and user feedback to inform content priorities.
  3. Audit fire-safety webpages for outdated, redundant or missing information.
  4. Develop a more intuitive hierarchy of topics, categories and subcategories.
  5. Create a sitemap and user flow to communicate the proposed structure.
  6. Collaborate on navigation and page-layout concepts.
  7. Revise and create web content in alignment with accessibility and municipal guidelines.
  8. Update navigation elements and support responsive website presentation.

01 / Research & discovery

I began by examining the existing digital experience to better understand where visitors might encounter difficulty accessing fire-safety information.

The research process involved reviewing website analytics and user feedback to identify pain points and help prioritize important information.

Key activities

  1. Reviewed web analytics to inform an understanding of user behaviour and content access.
  2. Examined user feedback to identify areas of confusion or navigation friction.
  3. Considered the relevance and importance of information when determining content priorities.

These findings informed the subsequent content audit and restructuring process.

02 / Content audit & evaluation

The next phase focused on understanding the existing content landscape before proposing changes to the website structure.

I inventoried the available fire-safety information and reviewed content to identify outdated or redundant material that required further revision or removal.

Key activities

  1. Reviewed existing fire-safety webpages and topics.
  2. Identified content that was outdated, repetitive or potentially unnecessary.
  3. Flagged information requiring revision or removal.
  4. Identified content gaps requiring additional information.
  5. Used the audit to inform a revised content hierarchy.

03 / Information architecture & user flow

Using the research and content audit, I reorganized the fire-safety information into a more logical structure.

This involved reviewing how topics related to one another, grouping information into appropriate categories and subcategories, and developing a sitemap to communicate the proposed hierarchy.

I used Whimsical to document the user flow and visualize the organization of information across the website.

Key activities

  1. Reorganized fire-safety topics based on relevance, importance and user needs.
  2. Established logical categories and subcategories.
  3. Developed a sitemap representing the proposed website structure.
  4. Created a user-flow diagram in Whimsical to communicate navigation pathways and content relationships.

Interactive fire safety user flow

04 / Wireframing & design collaboration

Once the content structure had been developed, I collaborated with designers to translate the proposed organization into the website's navigation and page-layout concepts.

This phase focused on connecting the information architecture with the actual experience of navigating the website.

Key activities

  1. Communicated the proposed sitemap and information hierarchy.
  2. Collaborated on how content categories would appear within navigation menus.
  3. Reviewed how the updated structure could be reflected in page layouts.
  4. Considered clarity and accessibility throughout the design process.

05 / Content updates & website implementation

The final phase involved translating the revised information architecture into updated web content and navigation.

I revised and streamlined existing content to improve clarity, conciseness and consistency with municipal content guidelines. I also created new material where information gaps had been identified.

Content improvements

  1. Revised existing information for clarity and readability.
  2. Streamlined content to reduce unnecessary complexity and repetition.
  3. Created new content to address identified information gaps.
  4. Applied accessibility standards and City content guidelines.

Navigation & digital experience

  1. Configured navigation menus to reflect the updated information hierarchy.
  2. Updated breadcrumbs and internal links to support navigation between related content.
  3. Supported responsive presentation across desktop and mobile devices.
  4. Worked within the website content management process to support consistent publishing.

Outcome & contribution

The project supported the reorganization and improvement of fire-safety information on the City of Mississauga's website.

My work contributed to a revised information structure, updated web content and navigation, and a more user-centred approach to presenting public safety information.

The project brought together research, website analytics, information architecture, accessibility, content design and collaboration with internal teams.

Key deliverables:

Content audit, revised information architecture, sitemap, Whimsical user flow, wireframing collaboration, updated webpage content and navigation improvements.

Project takeaway

This project demonstrated how research, user feedback and information architecture can inform improvements to public-facing digital services.

It also reinforced the importance of balancing user needs, accessibility requirements, content accuracy and organizational standards when managing municipal websites.
