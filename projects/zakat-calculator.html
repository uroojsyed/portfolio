Human Appeal Canada — Zakat Calculator Redesign

Using website analytics, UX research, and AI-assisted development to design and prototype a more transparent, streamlined Zakat Calculator.

Google Analytics 4 Behavioural analytics Data analysis UX/UI Figma Replit AI-assisted development Functional prototyping GitHub

Overview

Human Appeal Canada's Zakat Calculator enables donors to estimate their annual Zakat obligation using financial assets, eligible liabilities, and Nisab thresholds.

The project aimed to improve the existing experience through a more streamlined, transparent, and intuitive interface. A central objective was to reduce unnecessary interactions while giving users greater visibility into how their Zakat estimate is calculated.

My approach combined quantitative website analytics, behavioural analytics, UX evaluation, functional requirements development, and low-fidelity wireframing.

I then used AI-assisted development tools in Replit to translate my designs and requirements into a functional prototype, demonstrating how emerging technologies can support the progression from research and design to technical implementation.

01 / Defining the problem

Calculating Zakat requires users to understand different asset categories, eligible deductions, and the underlying calculation rules.

A multi-step calculator can make this process more difficult when users must enter information without seeing how it affects their estimate. This creates the potential for an opaque, or black-box, calculation experience.

The redesign focused on three opportunities:

  1. Efficiency: Reduce unnecessary steps and make relevant financial inputs easy to find.
  2. Transparency: Display an updating estimate with explanations of the calculation.
  3. Trust and clarity: Help users understand their results before proceeding toward giving.

The intended outcome was a clearer donor experience with less potential for friction. The actual effect on user completion and donation behaviour would require testing.

02 / Analyzing the existing user journey

To inform the redesign, I combined Google Analytics 4 acquisition data with Hotjar/Contentsquare behavioural analytics for the existing Zakat Calculator.

Rather than examining traffic and engagement as isolated metrics, I considered how users arrived at the calculator, how they interacted with the page, and where the experience presented opportunities for improvement.

The objective was to establish a data-informed rationale for simplifying the calculation process and making the user's financial information easier to understand.

Data sources: Google Analytics 4 (January 1–October 29, 2026) and Hotjar/Contentsquare (January 1–March 31, 2026). The reporting periods and measurement definitions differ. Figures are presented separately where necessary.

Finding 01 / Campaign-driven traffic

GA4 showed that 75.2% of recorded Zakat Calculator page views originated from paid search, representing 2,344 of 3,116 views.

This indicated that paid campaigns were an important source of traffic to the calculator.

As a result, the entry experience became a key design consideration. Visitors arriving through campaigns should be able to identify relevant financial inputs and begin a calculation without unnecessary navigation or decisions.

Design response: Prioritize clearly visible asset categories, simplified inputs, and a live Zakat estimate.

Finding 02 / User engagement and interaction

The Hotjar/Contentsquare analytics dashboard for the calculator recorded approximately 1,400 sessions and 7,300 clicks, with an average time on page of 1 minute and 30 seconds.

Average scroll depth was 67.9%, providing a measure of how far users progressed vertically through the page.

These metrics helped frame questions about the discoverability of financial inputs, supporting explanations, and donation actions.

For example, if relevant controls or explanations are positioned too far down the page, some visitors may not encounter them during their session. The scroll-depth average alone does not establish whether specific elements were seen.

Design response: Improve the information hierarchy, make important asset categories easier to locate, and keep the estimated Zakat amount visible as inputs change.

Finding 03 / Potential interaction friction

Behavioural analytics recorded 118 sessions with rage clicks and a reported drop-off rate of 72% in the filtered page overview.

Rage clicks involve repeated interactions with an element and may indicate confusion, unresponsive controls, or unmet user expectations.

These signals warranted closer investigation of the calculator's input controls and navigation. They did not establish which specific elements caused frustration or why users left.

The reported drop-off rate also required careful interpretation. Without a confirmed calculation completion event and the platform's precise metric definition, it cannot be treated as a 72% calculation abandonment rate.

Design response: Reduce unnecessary interactions, clarify input controls, provide contextual explanations, and make calculation feedback more immediate.

Connecting the findings

Considered together, the analytics described a calculator attracting substantial paid-search traffic, while behavioural reporting highlighted interaction patterns and potential friction that warranted further investigation.

This shaped the central redesign hypothesis: making financial inputs easier to discover, reducing unnecessary steps, and providing transparent real-time calculations could help users complete the process more confidently and efficiently.

The resulting priorities were:

  1. Improve the entry experience: Make common financial categories immediately discoverable.
  2. Reduce interaction friction: Simplify controls and remove avoidable decisions.
  3. Increase transparency: Show a continuously updating estimate and explain the calculation breakdown.
  4. Strengthen measurement: Track calculation starts, completed estimates, and donation progression to evaluate the redesign.

Analytical limitations

The analysis did not establish a complete historical conversion funnel or a causal relationship between specific interface elements and user drop-off.

These limitations informed the proposed event-tracking and validation framework developed later in the project.

03 / Translating insights into requirements

I organized the redesign requirements around reducing interaction friction, improving asset discoverability, increasing calculation transparency, and simplifying the giving process.

Reducing unnecessary steps

  1. Introduce a live Zakat estimate that updates as financial inputs change.
  2. Use CAD as the default calculation and reporting currency.
  3. Replace free-text account type entry with a dropdown including an Other option.
  4. Remove the separate lunar-year confirmation step in the proposed experience, subject to validation of the eligibility assumptions.

Improving asset discoverability

  1. Provide visible entry points for stocks and cryptocurrency.
  2. Include a dedicated foreign-currency entry area while retaining CAD totals.
  3. Group related assets and liabilities into clearly identified categories.
  4. Introduce information tooltips with supporting video explanations for individual input categories.

Increasing calculation transparency

  1. Display an updating summary of entered assets and liabilities.
  2. Explain how eligible wealth is determined from the entered values.
  3. Show how the 2.5% Zakat rate is applied to the relevant amount.
  4. Identify the selected Nisab threshold and explain its role in the calculation.
  5. Explore automatic Silver Nisab selection below the Gold threshold, subject to verification of the applicable calculation methodology.

Clarifying the giving experience

  1. Record interest received separately from the amount used to calculate Zakat.
  2. Exclude interest received from the Zakat calculation.
  3. Provide a separate donation action when a positive interest amount is entered.
  4. Present Zakat and interest donation amounts separately in the final review.
  5. Preserve Human Appeal Canada's branding and website navigation.

04 / From data to design decisions

To connect analysis with implementation, I mapped the available evidence and project requirements to specific design decisions and future evaluation metrics.

Acquisition data → Clearer entry experience

Evidence: Paid search accounted for 75.2% of recorded calculator page views.

Design response: Make relevant asset inputs immediately visible and provide a clear starting point.

Evaluation: Measure calculator start rates by acquisition channel.

Behavioural questions → Simplified interactions

Evidence: Behavioural analytics identified interaction signals that warranted further investigation.

Design response: Reduce unnecessary steps and clarify input controls and navigation.

Evaluation: Compare task completion, time to estimate, and input error rates.

UX requirements → Transparent calculations

Problem: The calculation process needed to provide greater visibility into how entered financial values affect the estimate.

Design response: Introduce a live estimate and an expandable calculation breakdown.

Evaluation: Test whether users can correctly explain how their Zakat was calculated.

Analytics limitations → Event-level measurement

Evidence: The available GA4 export did not establish calculation completion or donation progression.

Design response: Define a calculator-specific conversion funnel and interaction event taxonomy.

Evaluation: Assess event coverage, completion rate, and donation progression after implementation.

06 / Measuring the redesigned experience

An important analytical limitation was the absence of a complete calculator journey in the available historical reports.

I therefore proposed a measurement framework that could evaluate calculator engagement, completion, and donation progression after implementation.

Proposed conversion funnel

  1. Calculator viewed: A user enters the calculator page.
  2. Calculation started: The user begins entering financial information.
  3. Estimate reviewed: A valid calculation reaches the review stage.
  4. Donation initiated: The user proceeds to the donation journey.
  5. Donation completed: A verified donation is completed and, where possible, attributed to the calculator journey.

Proposed funnel only. Historical counts for the individual stages were not available in the reviewed analytics reports.

Proposed performance indicators

  1. Calculator start rate: Sessions containing a calculation start divided by calculator page sessions.
  2. Calculation completion rate: Valid completed estimates divided by started calculations.
  3. Step abandonment rate: The share of journeys that do not progress to the next defined stage.
  4. Median time to estimate: The median elapsed time between calculation start and valid review.
  5. Donation progression rate: Completed calculations followed by donation initiation divided by completed calculations.
  6. Help interaction rate: Calculator sessions with explanatory content interactions divided by calculator sessions.

Proposed GA4 event tracking

Calculator-specific events would provide more useful information about user progression than page views alone.

  1. calculator_start — first meaningful interaction.
  2. asset_category_selected — selection of a predefined financial category.
  3. calculator_help_opened — interaction with explanatory content.
  4. calculation_error_displayed — predefined validation error category.
  5. calculation_review_viewed — valid calculation reviewed.
  6. donation_flow_started — user continues toward giving.
  7. donation_completed — verified donation completion, where reliable attribution and consent permit.

Events would need clearly defined triggering conditions and validation before being used for reporting.

Defining redesign success

The proposed evaluation framework connects each design objective with a measurable outcome.

  1. Efficiency: Reduce median time to estimate without compromising accuracy.
  2. Completion: Increase the calculation completion rate.
  3. Transparency: Improve users' ability to explain their calculated result.
  4. Input clarity: Reduce input errors and increase task success.
  5. Donation progression: Increase progression from a completed estimate to giving.
  6. Measurement quality: Establish reliable tracking across the defined funnel.

Privacy-conscious measurement

Because the calculator handles financial information, analytics should measure interactions without capturing individual asset balances, interest amounts, or personally identifiable data.

Reporting should use appropriately aggregated information and follow applicable consent and privacy requirements.

07 / Validation and next steps

Before implementation, the functional prototype would require further usability, accessibility, and calculation validation.

  1. Test whether users can find relevant asset categories and complete realistic tasks.
  2. Evaluate whether the live estimate and breakdown improve understanding.
  3. Test calculation logic using predefined fictional financial scenarios and expected results.
  4. Verify Nisab thresholds, eligibility assumptions, foreign-currency handling, and other calculation rules with appropriate subject-matter reviewers.
  5. Review keyboard accessibility, form labels, tooltip behaviour, and error announcements.
  6. Validate event tracking before implementation and compare performance using appropriately controlled reporting periods.

Outcome & status

The project progressed from quantitative analytics and UX requirements development to low-fidelity wireframes and an interactive calculator prototype.

I combined GA4 acquisition analysis, behavioural analytics, and usability considerations to identify opportunities for simplifying the calculation process and improving transparency.

Using AI-assisted development through Replit, I translated wireframes and functional specifications into a working demonstration of the proposed experience. The implementation is also documented through the project's GitHub repository.

I also developed a proposed measurement framework covering conversion funnels, interaction events, success criteria, and privacy-conscious evaluation.

Together, these deliverables demonstrate a multidisciplinary workflow connecting data analysis, UX design, requirements engineering, AI-assisted development, and performance evaluation.

Project status: Analytics review, requirements development, Figma wireframes, and functional prototype. The redesigned calculator is not presented as a production launch. Independent calculation validation and post-launch performance assessment remain future work.
